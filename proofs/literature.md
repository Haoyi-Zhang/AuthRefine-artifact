# Literature comparison and reference counts

The comparison is purposive and organized around the actual obligations: bidirectional representation, authorization, refinement, certificate translation, dependence, and proof/implementation boundaries. It is not a census or a ranking of the journal. The 22 distinct full-paper comparisons are recorded in `literature_comparison.csv`: twelve TOPLAS methodological comparators in the historical sample, five separately selected high-visibility journal articles, and five adjacent-venue papers. Bibliographic metadata and the exact readable-copy locations are recorded in `external_resources.csv` and `literature_reference_counts.csv`.

This historical comparison sample predates the current TOSEM submission direction. It is retained as scholarly background, not presented as TOSEM venue calibration or an acceptance-quality score.

## Direct competitors and the retained distinction

Margrave (Fisler et al., ICSE 2005) is a direct policy-difference comparator, not merely background on access control. It uses decision diagrams to analyze authorization and the semantic impact of changes. Dougherty, Fisler and Krishnamurthi (IJCAR 2006) are closer to the stateful question: contextual containment is restricted to accessible policy environments. Their argument already connects reachable local judgments with executions. The present result does not originate that principle. It assumes a common target graph whose observations reconstruct the entire source state for every admitted binding, then characterizes each binding's earliest discrepancy by minimum target distance.

DynPAL (Becker, CSF 2009) supports policy-changing actions, reachability and invariants in a richer update language. UCON (Park and Sandhu, 2004) treats mutable attributes and ongoing usage conditions. Zelkova (Backes et al., FMCAD 2018) and Block Public Access (Bouchet et al., ESEC/FSE 2020) show that semantic permission comparison and query-relative simplification are established authorization-analysis techniques. Cedar's published PACMPL 2024 article supplies a typed, side-effect-free language and symbolic analysis. Its publication replaces the earlier preprint citation; the work is counted once.

NetKAT supplies semantic and algebraic foundations for network equivalence. Its later coalgebraic decision procedure, symbolic language-equivalence algorithms, and bisimulation up to congruence provide algorithmic alternatives to exhaustive products. KATch (PACMPL 2024) adds a modern symbolic implementation. Relational NetKAT (Xu et al., PACMPL 2026) uses a specified relation to describe network changes, including changes to packet traces that are not equal-length lockstep transformations. The present equality criterion does not subsume that trace-relation language. Conversely, those publications do not automatically establish the fixed-reference failure-profile theorem for this observation model. The comparison is semantic and methodological, not a claimed performance ranking across incompatible workloads.

The manuscript's Related Work section groups the comparisons by what each approach checks. The citation locations and supporting claims are recorded in `reference_audit.csv`. The other publications support distinct obligations: state refinement and auxiliary variables; proof-carrying code and translation validation; formal controlled syntax; abstraction, counterexample refinement and dependence; or the distinction between preservation, rights safety and enforcement. The journal-calibration sample remains documented even when a calibration paper is not needed in the manuscript bibliography.

## Reading depth and publication metadata

The citation audit distinguishes a main-text passage from an abstract-only reading and from an inherited source-reading record. It does not turn a publication record into a claim of having verified every theorem. Newly inspected full-text passages include the dynamic-policy, Margrave, DynPAL, access-calculus, Zelkova, KATch, complete-state/trace-set and Relational NetKAT comparisons. Some foundational comparisons use a publisher or author-written abstract for a correspondingly narrow statement; those rows say so explicitly. Existing source-verification records are retained rather than presented as newly repeated readings.

Publication metadata takes precedence over an incidental date or footer in an author copy. The Existence of Refinement Mappings is cited as the 1991 journal article, while its available author report is an earlier access copy. The access-control calculus is TOPLAS 15(4), despite an inconsistent author-copy footer. Guarded Kleene Algebra with Tests belongs to the PACMPL 2020 POPL issue although online availability preceded that issue. Cedar's published author list and article identifier are used. Preprints still cited as preprints, including CNL-P and AutoCedar, are not assigned an unverified peer-reviewed status. No inaccessible page count or DOI is invented.

## Current method and executable evidence

Refinement mappings supply the transition relation used after an agreeing prefix. Breadth-first search supplies minimum target distances. Family-based verification and decision diagrams supply established ways to share work across fixed configurations. Classen et al. already compute the full set of failing products (Definition 18 and Theorem 19); returning all failed configurations is therefore not the distinguishing result. AuthRefine connects family classification to the shortest replay of each fixed binding. Its full-state observation condition makes the earliest mismatch a distance in one shared target graph. Active support groups share reference evaluations, retain correlations in the legal family, retire a binding at its first mismatch and replay that witness under the same complete binding. The caller product retains request history when the admitted input language restricts operations.

The argument and the implementations have corresponding evidence. `SUPPORT_INDEXED_FAMILY.md` states the active-family invariant; `PROTOCOL_GROUPING.md` extends it to caller histories. The independent overlap and caller audits check complete profiles, correlated subfamilies and every replay field. Two complete symbolic implementations, a finite-domain decision diagram and a CUDD backend, calculate disjoint first-failure regions for the same supplied families. The equal-output comparison in `results/symbolic-comparison/` measures six algorithms on 158 tasks, with seven rotated process repetitions. It exposes semantic sharing that conservative support misses, as well as the common cost of producing individual replays. Decision diagrams and CUDD are comparison mechanisms, not themselves a novelty claim for this project.

The interpretation-family result separates uniform agreement, agreement of a proper nonempty subfamily and disagreement of every admitted interpretation. In the last case, no supplied correspondence explains the observed interface; it does not follow that the source specification is ambiguous. A known correct fixed-binding compilation retains that binding. These classifications guide review without changing the supplied requirement or silently enlarging its family.

Application evidence now includes native Piccolo session operations through the ORM and SQLite and native Conet policy operations through Casbin and its file adapter. The session study executes complete reachable stores for two through six sessions, compares all supplied bijections under three caller languages, and replays the selected excluding words. Rich observations separately test selected-row identity and the premises of a one-to-one family. The policy-file study follows successful changes through a new-instance reload and executes a persistence repair under the same contract. Source-body, prospective-transfer and published-patch records provide other integration evidence; the six public model/policy pairs remain one earlier cohort rather than the full application evidence.

These sources answer different questions. Native records establish behavior of the selected application interfaces; independent equations test the diagnostic interpretation of those records; controlled cost tasks isolate graph, support and output costs. The applications do not estimate the prevalence of unresolved correspondence families in developer reviews. The Lean files check the stated conditional laws and finite components, while the executable comparators are checked by independent computations and sensitivity controls. Current TOSEM genre and submission guidance is documented separately in `tosem-calibration.md`.
